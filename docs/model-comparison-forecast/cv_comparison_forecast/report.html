Walk-forward CV comparison

MAE / MAPE use the median (point) prediction; pinball-d2 uses the 5%, 50% and 95% quantiles and coverage / width the 5%-95% interval, for every model whatever other quantile levels it predicts ("-" = model has no quantiles). All rows across the 8 CV folds are pooled.

operator_day_ahead is the ENTSO-E day-ahead load forecast for France (issued by RTE), shown for the 24h horizon only: its forecast for target time t is compared with the actual load at t. It has no quantiles or fit time, so it is absent from the pinball, coverage/width and Pareto figures.

Note on recalibration: post-hoc quantile recalibration is not uniformly beneficial. It improves HGB (hgb_quantile_q7 -> hgb_quantile_recal_q7, 90% coverage ~75% -> ~90%) but degrades TabICL (tabicl_n8_full -> tabicl_recal_n8_full: MAE +115 MW, MAPE +0.20 pp, 90% coverage 87% -> 85%). The recalibrator learns its corrections from inner walk-forward folds in which TabICL is refit on much less training context than the final model, so the learned shifts do not transfer. See results/model_comparison/tabicl_n8_full_forecast_safe_recal/README.md. Note on weather (2026-11-23): chronos2_ctx4000 and t0_gamma are the day1-stitched reruns (results/model_comparison/chronos2_ctx4000_day1_stitched and t0_gamma_day1_stitched): for the whole input window, like the per-horizon models, they get the 24h-lead (day1) weather forecast for the past and for future steps up to 18h, and the 48h-lead (day2) forecast for later steps. The previous runs gave them day2 weather everywhere; the paired differences (new - old, MAE average: t0 +2.6 MW [-5.6, +10.0], Chronos -6.1 MW [-13.5, +0.5]) are not significant. tabicl_forecast_ctx1024_n4 still gets day2 weather for the whole window, and kumo_forecast uses past-only oracle weather.

Paired differences vs baseline

Each row is a model, with one lane per horizon. MAE/MAPE are relative to hgb_mean; pinball/width are relative to hgb_quantile_q7. Large markers: pooled mean difference over all test rows, with a 95% moving-block bootstrap interval (14-day blocks over daily differences; conditional on the fitted models, it ignores training randomness). Small dots: mean difference in each of the 8 CV folds (one dot per fold; hover shows the fold). Folds are not independent, so no t-interval across folds is shown. The dashed zero line means no difference; lower is better for every panel (losses and width). A model whose interval excludes zero and whose dots are all on one side is consistently different from the baseline; dots on both sides of zero mean the sign depends on the period.

How the uncertainty intervals are computed
  1. Per-row difference. For every CV test row, compute the row-level loss for the model and its baseline (MAPE difference in percentage points; pinball or interval width in MW), then diff = model − baseline.
  2. Pooled estimate. The large marker is mean(diff) over all test rows.
  3. 95% interval (moving-block bootstrap). Collapse diff to daily means, then resample 2000 times by drawing contiguous 14-day blocks of daily means and take the mean of each resample. The 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of those 2000 means form the interval, shifted to be centred on the pooled row mean. The 14-day blocks preserve autocorrelation in the load errors (they are correlated over consecutive hours and days), so the interval is not artificially narrow the way an IID resample of rows would be.
  4. Per-fold dots. The small dots are the mean difference within each of the 8 CV folds.

The interval is conditional on the fitted models (it ignores training randomness) and only covers the test range. The folds are not independent (nested training sets, adjacent test blocks), so no t-interval across folds is shown.

Coverage vs interval width

Each point is one model/horizon, using the mean coverage and mean interval width across the 8 folds. The vertical dashed line is the nominal 90% coverage. Points close to the line are well calibrated; lower is sharper.
